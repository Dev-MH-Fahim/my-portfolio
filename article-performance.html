Performance · 5 min read

A practical first pass at a faster website.

A useful performance improvement starts with finding the actual bottleneck—not installing another optimization plugin by default.

A practical field note · Review date: 2026

Performance is experienced by people, not just measured by a score. A page that responds quickly, stays stable as it loads, and makes its main content available early feels better to use.

Measure a representative page

Check a few real pages on mobile and desktop. Look at the main content’s loading, layout shifts, and interaction responsiveness. A homepage score alone may hide slower product, article, or contact pages.

Right-size the images

Use image dimensions that match the display area, choose modern formats when your publishing workflow supports them, and avoid loading a large original where a smaller derivative will do. Keep meaningful alternative text for informative images.

Remove unnecessary work

Review third-party scripts, duplicate plugins, heavy animation, and resources loaded site-wide that only one page needs. Remove what the site no longer uses and measure again.

Protect layout stability

Reserve space for images, embeds, and other content that arrives later. Avoid unexpected font swaps and injected content that pushes the page around while someone is reading.

Check the result under real conditions

Test with a slower connection and a mid-range device if possible. Compare the same pages before and after each meaningful change; performance wins should not come at the cost of accessibility or functionality.

A reliable baseline and one measured fix beat a stack of untested optimizations.
